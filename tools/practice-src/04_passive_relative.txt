The Charles Bridge in Prague, which connects the Old Town with the Lesser Town, was built in the 14th century. It was started in 1357 during the reign of Charles IV, who gave the bridge its later name. For centuries it was the only way across the river. The statues which line the bridge were added much later, mostly around 1700. Today the original statues are kept in a museum and the ones that visitors see are copies. The bridge is crossed by thousands of tourists every day, which is why many locals avoid it.
@q ## When was the bridge started? ## in 1357 / around 1700 / in the 19th century ## in 1357
@q ## Where are the original statues today? ## in a museum / on the bridge / in a church ## in a museum
@q ## Why do many locals avoid the bridge? ## It is too crowded. / It is dangerous. / It is closed at night. ## It is too crowded.
@end
